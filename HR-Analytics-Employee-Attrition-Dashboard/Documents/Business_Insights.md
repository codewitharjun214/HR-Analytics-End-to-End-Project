# Business Insights

## Insight 1
Overall Employee Attrition Rate: 16.12%

## Insight 2
Department-wise attrition analysis shows that Research & Developmenthas the highest employee attrition rate.

## Insight 3
Employees who work overtime have a significantly higher attrition rate than employees who do not work overtime. This indicates that overtime may be a major contributor to employee turnover.

## Insight 4
Employees with low job satisfaction have the highest attrition rate (22.84%), while employees with very high job satisfaction have the lowest attrition rate (11.33%). This suggests that improving employee job satisfaction may help reduce attrition.

## Insight 5

Employees in the 18–25 age group have the highest attrition rate. This indicates that younger employees are more likely to leave the organization. HR should strengthen onboarding, mentoring, career development, and engagement programs to improve retention among early-career employees.

## Insight 6

Employees earning between 0–5K have the highest attrition rate (21.76%), while employees earning 15K–20K have the lowest attrition rate (3.76%). This suggests that salary may influence employee retention, and improving compensation for lower-income employees could help reduce attrition.

## Insight 7

Employees with the shortest tenure have the highest attrition rate, indicating that new employees are more likely to leave the organization. HR should strengthen onboarding, mentoring, and early career engagement programs to improve retention during the first few years.

## Insight 8

Employees working in the <Job Role> role have the highest attrition rate. HR should investigate workload, compensation, career progression, and role-specific challenges to improve retention within this department.

## Insight 9

Employees with poor work-life balance experience a higher attrition rate compared to employees with better work-life balance. Improving flexible work policies and employee well-being initiatives may help reduce employee turnover.

## Insight 10

Employees with lower environment satisfaction have a higher attrition rate. Creating a positive work environment, improving workplace culture, and increasing employee engagement may help improve retention.

## Insight 11

Employees who travel frequently for business have a higher attrition rate than employees who travel rarely or do not travel. HR should evaluate travel policies, workload, and employee well-being to reduce travel-related turnover.

## Insight 12

Attrition varies across different marital status groups. HR should understand the specific needs of each employee group and design appropriate retention strategies based on employee demographics.

## Insight 13

Employee attrition is comparable across gender groups, indicating that gender alone may not be the primary factor influencing employee turnover. HR should focus on other organizational and job-related factors.

## Insight 14

Employees from the <Education Field> education field have the highest attrition rate. HR should further investigate career expectations, job-role alignment, and professional development opportunities for employees from this educational background.
